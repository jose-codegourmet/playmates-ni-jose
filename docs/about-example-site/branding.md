# PawPair — Brand Guidelines

## 1. Brand Overview

**Brand name:** PawPair  
**Category:** Pet social discovery and matchmaking app  
**Tagline:** **Better matches. Happier tails.**  
**Secondary line:** Find the right playmates, walking buddies, and pet-parent friends nearby.

PawPair is a fictional consumer app that brings dating-app-style discovery to the pet community. Pet parents create profiles for their pets, browse compatible nearby companions, match, chat, and arrange safe playdates or group activities.

The brand should feel modern, energetic, trustworthy, and emotionally warm. It must look polished enough to showcase a premium Next.js marketing template while still feeling playful and approachable.

> PawPair is about social compatibility, friendship, exercise, and community. It should never be positioned as a breeding marketplace.

---

## 2. Brand Purpose

### Mission

Help pets live happier, healthier, and more social lives by making it easy for their humans to find compatible local companions.

### Vision

A world where every pet has a trusted circle of playmates, walking buddies, and humans who understand their needs.

### Brand Promise

PawPair makes meeting the right pet friends feel simple, safe, and fun.

---

## 3. Positioning

### One-Sentence Positioning

PawPair is a local pet matchmaking app that helps pet parents discover compatible playmates and arrange safer, better social experiences.

### Elevator Pitch

Not every dog enjoys the same kind of play, and not every pet parent wants to approach strangers at the park. PawPair uses pet profiles, preferences, temperament, energy level, and location to help people find better matches before meeting in person.

### Key Differentiators

- Compatibility based on more than distance
- Profiles built around temperament, size, age, and energy
- Safety-first meetups and community guidelines
- Match, chat, and plan in one place
- Designed for pets and their humans
- Friendly local community discovery

---

## 4. Target Audience

### Primary Audience

Pet parents aged approximately 22–40 who:

- Treat their pets like family
- Live in cities or active suburban communities
- Use modern social and dating apps
- Want their pets to exercise and socialize
- Care about safety and compatibility
- Enjoy visually polished lifestyle products

### Secondary Audience

- New pet owners seeking guidance and community
- People who recently moved to a new city
- Owners of shy, senior, or highly energetic pets
- Dog walkers, trainers, groomers, and pet-friendly businesses
- Rescue organizations and adoption communities

### Audience Mindset

Users should feel:

- “My pet deserves friends who match their personality.”
- “I want a safer way to meet other pet owners.”
- “I want something easier than random park encounters.”
- “This feels fun, but it also feels responsible.”

## 5. Brand Personality

PawPair should balance five traits:

| Trait | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Playful | Light, expressive, and joyful without becoming childish |
| Friendly | Warm, welcoming, and community-oriented |
| Smart | Compatibility and product decisions feel thoughtful |
| Trustworthy | Safety, consent, and responsible pet ownership matter |
| Modern | Clean layouts, strong typography, and app-inspired interactions |

### Personality Spectrum

- More **playful** than corporate
- More **premium** than cartoonish
- More **warm** than technical
- More **confident** than loud
- More **inclusive** than exclusive

---

## 6. Voice and Tone

### Core Voice

PawPair speaks like a pet-loving friend who understands product design. The voice is conversational, encouraging, clever, and clear.

### Voice Principles

#### Warm, not overly sentimental

Use:

> Every great friendship starts with a good sniff.

Avoid:

> Your fur baby’s heart has been waiting for destiny.

#### Playful, not childish

Use:

> Swipe less randomly. Match more thoughtfully.

Avoid:

> Wittle floofers finding bestest fwiends!

#### Clear, not technical

Use:

> Match by energy, size, temperament, and distance.

Avoid:

> Our proprietary multivariable compatibility engine evaluates pet metadata.

#### Safety-conscious, not alarming

Use:

> Meet in public, verify profiles, and stay in control of every introduction.

Avoid:

> Other pet apps are dangerous.

## 7. Messaging Pillars

### 7.1 Better Compatibility

Matching should feel intentional. The product considers traits such as:

- Species
- Breed or mix
- Size
- Age
- Energy level
- Temperament
- Play style
- Social comfort
- Distance
- Availability

**Messaging example:**  
“Find pets who can actually keep up—or know when to slow down.”

### 7.2 Easier Discovery

Users can discover suitable pets and people without awkward park introductions.

**Messaging example:**  
“See who is nearby before you grab the leash.”

### 7.3 Safer Meetups

The platform promotes public meetups, clear expectations, profile verification, and owner-controlled communication.

**Messaging example:**  
“Get to know the human before the first nose-to-nose hello.”

### 7.4 Stronger Local Community

PawPair is not only for one-to-one matches. It can also surface walks, groups, events, and pet-friendly places.

**Messaging example:**  
“Build a local pack that feels right for both of you.”

## 9. Visual Direction

### Creative Concept

**A polished social app wrapped in an expressive pet lifestyle brand.**

The visual language should combine:

- Bold editorial typography
- Rounded app cards
- Pet profile interfaces
- Friendly photography
- Soft gradients
- Playful stickers and icons
- Spacious, premium layouts
- Subtle motion inspired by swiping and matching

The website should feel suitable for a modern startup launch, not a generic pet store.

## 11. Typography

Use Google Fonts through `next/font/google`.

### Recommended Pairing

#### Display: Fraunces

Use for:

- Hero headlines
- Editorial callouts
- Large testimonial quotes
- Select campaign headings

Fraunces adds personality and warmth without feeling overly decorative.

#### Interface and Body: Manrope

Use for:

- Navigation
- Buttons
- Paragraphs
- Labels
- Pricing
- App UI previews
- Form fields

Manrope feels modern, readable, and product-focused.

### Alternative Pairing

- **Display:** DM Serif Display
- **Body:** Plus Jakarta Sans

### Type Scale

| Role | Suggested Size |
| --- | --- |
| Hero display | `clamp(3.25rem, 8vw, 7rem)` |
| Page title | `clamp(2.5rem, 6vw, 5rem)` |
| Section heading | `clamp(2rem, 4vw, 3.75rem)` |
| Card title | `1.25rem–1.5rem` |
| Body large | `1.125rem–1.25rem` |
| Body | `1rem` |
| Caption | `0.8125rem–0.875rem` |

### Typography Rules

- Headlines may be tightly tracked.
- Body copy should have generous line height.
- Avoid using the display font for UI controls.
- Keep paragraph widths around 55–70 characters.
- Use sentence case for buttons and headings.
- Avoid all-caps paragraphs.

## 12. Logo Direction

### Primary Logo Concept

A simple PawPair wordmark paired with an icon that combines:

- Two overlapping pet profile shapes
- A paw print
- A subtle heart or match spark
- Two tails forming a connection

### Logo Personality

- Rounded but not bubbly
- Recognizable at app-icon size
- Works in one color
- Avoids generic dog-bone imagery
- Suitable for both dogs and cats

### App Icon

A compact symbol using:

- Coral background
- White or cream icon
- Two connected paw/profile forms
- Rounded-square container

Do not use detailed animal illustrations inside the logo.

## 13. UI Design Language

### Shape

- Cards: `20px–32px` radius
- Buttons: pill or softly rounded
- Inputs: `14px–18px` radius
- Avatars: circular
- Images: rounded with occasional organic masks
- Badges: compact pills

### Borders and Shadows

Use soft borders and low-opacity shadows.

```css
border: 1px solid rgba(23, 21, 31, 0.08);
box-shadow: 0 24px 80px rgba(49, 35, 62, 0.12);
```

Avoid heavy black outlines unless used as a deliberate editorial accent.

### Cards

Core card types include:

- Pet profile card
- Compatibility score card
- Feature card
- Review card
- Pricing card
- Blog card
- Safety tip card
- Local event card

### Buttons

#### Primary

Coral fill, dark or white text depending on contrast.

Suggested labels:

- Find a playmate
- Join the pack
- Create a pet profile
- Start matching
- Try PawPair free

#### Secondary

Cream or transparent background with a visible border.

Suggested labels:

- See how it works
- Explore features
- Read our safety guide
- View plans

### Icon Style

- Rounded line icons
- Consistent stroke weight
- Minimal detail
- Occasional filled accent
- Use paw, spark, shield, map pin, chat, leash, calendar, and profile motifs

## 14. Photography Direction

### Required Feeling

Photography should feel candid, social, bright, and emotionally real.

### Image Subjects

- Two compatible dogs interacting calmly
- Pet parents meeting in a public park
- Cats observing one another from comfortable distances
- Small group walks
- Senior pets with gentle companions
- High-energy dogs playing outdoors
- Urban pet parents using a phone
- Close-up pet portraits for profile cards

### Composition

- Leave negative space for text
- Prefer eye-level pet photography
- Mix wide lifestyle scenes with clean portraits
- Include varied breeds, sizes, ages, and pet parents
- Show visible leashes or responsible supervision where relevant

### Avoid

- Studio-stock images with pure white backgrounds
- Costumes that make the product feel childish
- Aggressive or uncontrolled interactions
- Breeding-related visuals
- Only featuring puppies
- Overly staged “perfect family” scenes
- Excessive heart graphics

## 16. Motion Direction

Use Framer Motion for purposeful interaction.

### Recommended Motion

- Profile cards gently stack and swipe
- Match badge pops into view
- Avatars float subtly
- Sections fade and rise on scroll
- Compatibility meters animate once
- Marquee of pet profiles moves slowly
- CTA arrows shift slightly on hover
- Mobile menu slides in with a soft spring

### Motion Rules

- Keep durations between 180–500 ms for UI movement.
- Avoid constant bouncing.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Do not autoplay loud video.
- Use motion to explain matching and discovery.

---

## 17. Responsive Behavior

The design must be mobile-first.

### Mobile

- One-column layouts
- Full-width CTA buttons where helpful
- Swipeable profile cards
- Compact navigation with sheet menu
- Simplified decorative graphics
- Pricing cards stacked vertically

### Tablet

- Two-column feature layouts
- Offset editorial cards
- Expanded app-preview compositions

### Desktop

- Large editorial hero
- Layered profile cards
- Alternating content sections
- Three-column features and pricing
- Wider social-proof displays

Avoid fixed widths that cause horizontal overflow.

---

## 18. Accessibility

- Meet WCAG AA color contrast.
- Do not rely on color alone for match status.
- Provide alt text for all meaningful pet photography.
- Use visible keyboard focus states.
- Maintain logical heading order.
- Ensure motion can be reduced.
- Buttons must use descriptive labels.
- Form fields must have persistent labels.
- Profile controls should be screen-reader friendly.
- Do not place essential text inside images.

## 20. Brand Do and Don’t

### Do

- Show real pet personality.
- Keep layouts expressive and spacious.
- Explain compatibility clearly.
- Make safety visible throughout the journey.
- Use humor in small, smart doses.
- Present PawPair as a modern product.
- Include cats, dogs, and varied pet lifestyles.

### Don’t

- Make the site look like a veterinary clinic.
- Use childish copy everywhere.
- Imply guaranteed compatibility.
- Position pets as products.
- Focus on animal breeding.
- Use dark patterns around subscriptions.
- Overload every section with paw-print decorations.
- Sacrifice usability for novelty.

---

## 21. Brand Summary

PawPair should feel like a thoughtful social app, a joyful pet brand, and a credible startup at the same time.

The final visual balance is:

**60% polished consumer technology**  
**25% warm pet lifestyle**  
**15% playful personality**

Every page should reinforce the same idea:

> Better social experiences begin with a better match.
